## What I work on

The 21 cm line is the only practical probe that can map cosmic history in three dimensions from the present day back to *z* ∼ 30. It reaches into the “cosmic desert” that lies between the Cosmic Microwave Background at *z* ∼ 1100 and the optical galaxy surveys that stop near *z* ∼ 2, covering the Dark Ages, Cosmic Dawn, reionization, and the era when dark energy took over the expansion.

Getting there is hard for one reason above all others. Galactic and extragalactic foregrounds are four to five orders of magnitude brighter than the signal, and they are coupled to a chromatic instrument response through the primary beam, mutual coupling between elements, and time- and frequency-dependent gains. Most of my work is the analysis chain that separates the two: calibration, RFI mitigation, beam characterisation, foreground filtering, and the validation framework that decides whether a detection is real rather than an artefact.

## Questions driving the programme

- Is dark energy a cosmological constant, or is it evolving? Can 21 cm baryon acoustic oscillations test this independently of optical surveys, and place competitive bounds on the sum of the neutrino masses?
- What was the thermal state of the intergalactic medium during reionization, and can the 21 cm forest measure it before SKA-Low is on sky?
- How much cold neutral gas did galaxies hold at cosmic noon, and what regulated it?
- How do you recover a signal buried far beneath the systematics that produce it, and how do you know when you have?

## Current directions

**Precision cosmology with 21 cm intensity mapping.** The CHIME detection establishes that the auto-power spectrum is measurable at non-linear scales. The next step is the linear, BAO-scale modes, which are currently sacrificed to foreground filtering. Recovering them requires solving the instrumental systematics that limit every 21 cm experiment. I am extending the analysis to CHIME's lower-frequency band (400–600 MHz, *z* ∼ 1.4–2.5) and to the full archival dataset, and building the calibration and simulation infrastructure that CHORD will need to do the same an order of magnitude more sensitively.

**The thermal history of reionization through the 21 cm forest.** Absorption against bright high-redshift radio sources traces cold neutral gas directly along the line of sight and reaches small spatial scales that no other 21 cm technique can access. I co-led the first constraints on the thermal state of the neutral IGM at *z* ≈ 5.6 using the one-dimensional forest power spectrum. The uGMRT is a capable pathfinder at 120–250 MHz while SKA-Low is under construction, and I am developing the analysis framework and simulation infrastructure the technique will need.

**Cold gas at cosmic noon with CHIME HI absorbers.** Only about 150 extragalactic 21 cm absorbers are known, most of them at *z* < 1 and fewer than ten above *z* = 2. CHIME watches the entire northern sky continuously across 400–800 MHz, a band that ASKAP, MeerKAT and FAST all miss above *z* ∼ 1. Turning blind CHIME discoveries into a characterised catalogue through high-resolution follow-up would enlarge the *z* > 1 sample by a factor of five to ten and open up kinematic studies of cold gas at the peak of cosmic star formation.

**Neutral gas in individual galaxies through gravitational lensing.** The 21 cm line is the only direct tracer of a galaxy's atomic gas reservoir, and it is far too faint to detect from an individual galaxy at high redshift with today's telescopes. Strong lensing solves this: a foreground mass concentration can boost the signal by factors of ten to a hundred. Using the uGMRT, [I reported the first 5σ detection of HI 21 cm emission from a star-forming galaxy at *z* ≈ 1.3](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stac3696), magnified by an early-type elliptical at *z* ≈ 0.13. The measured atomic-to-stellar mass ratio, *M*<sub>HI</sub>/*M*<sub>∗</sub> ∼ 2.4, is far above the value in local star-forming galaxies (∼0.35), direct evidence that gas reservoirs have been depleted since cosmic noon. That detection took a modest amount of telescope time, which means the approach scales. I am now working to build it into a systematic programme across *z* = 0.5–1.5 using galaxy-galaxy lenses and the much larger Einstein radii of galaxy clusters, ahead of SKA-Mid, which should reach more than 10⁴ lensed HI galaxies.

## Background

Before Toronto I was a Trottier Chair Fellow in the Department of Physics and the Trottier Space Institute at McGill University (2021–2026), where I joined CHIME and led the auto-power spectrum detection. I completed my PhD at IIT Indore in 2021, where I built the wideband calibration, imaging and power-spectrum pipeline for the upgraded GMRT and used it to place the first post-upgrade constraints on the post-reionization 21 cm signal. I lead the *Akashganga* group within SKA-India and am a member of the SKAO Epoch of Reionization and Cosmology science working groups.
